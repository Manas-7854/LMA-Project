## 7. Leakage findings (important)

1. **`claim_id` encodes source site and label.** Every record with `claim_id < 8936` (4,151 records; 3,333 train /
   435 dev / 383 test) is labelled `true` — these are wire-news items (Reuters/AP-style headlines). PolitiFact,
   Snopes and TruthOrFiction occupy distinct higher id ranges. A majority-label-per-500-id-bin rule learned on train
   reaches **macro-F1 0.594 / accuracy 0.684 on test**. `claim_id` must never be a model input, and must not appear
   in any id the model sees. (The first smoke run exposed corpus ids built from `claim_id`; ids are now opaque hashes
   and passages are shown to the model as `E1..Ek`.)
2. **The "true" class is dominated by a different genre.** At least 4,151 of 6,306 `true` records (66%, the id
   block above) are news headlines whose main_text is the news article reporting that very headline;
   `false`/`mixture`/`unproven` come mostly from fact-check articles. 2,000 of the 2,744 records without a
   fact-checker are `true`, and `unproven` never occurs for PolitiFact, Health News Review or no-fact-checker records. Article *style* therefore predicts the label: TF-IDF + logistic regression on
   verdict-masked main_text alone gets **macro-F1 0.576 (test)**; on the claim alone **0.482**; on metadata
   (fact-checkers, subjects, source availability) **0.495**. Any LLM system must be compared against these numbers.
3. **Verdict statements inside main_text.** The rating word has already been stripped from PolitiFact articles
   ("We rate it ."), but the verdict sentence frame and the Truth-O-Meter definitions remain
   ("…contains an element of truth but ignores critical facts…"). Verdict-pattern sentences occur in 10.4% (train),
   15.1% (dev), 12.6% (test) of articles, more often for false (17.9%) than true (6.7%). These sentences are masked
   (`src/oblig/data/masking.py`): 1,811 sentences removed, touching 11.3% of articles.
4. **Explanation ↔ main_text overlap.** The explanation (gold justification) appears verbatim in main_text for 4.7%
   of records, and at least one explanation sentence appears in main_text for 13.4%. The explanation is therefore a
   *silver evidence* signal for those records, and must never be a model input.
5. **Label semantics differ by source.** Health News Review records label the *news story's quality*
   ("This story clearly and accurately summarizes…"), not the factual truth of the headline. This is a dataset
   ambiguity to report, not to fix.

## 9. Recommended evaluation protocol (draft — see docs/EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md)

* Official splits; 37 malformed rows excluded and counted.
* Model input: claim text only. Evidence: retrieved passages from a pooled corpus of verdict-masked main_text of
  all 12,251 records. Forbidden inputs: label, explanation, claim_id, fact_checkers, subjects, sources, date.
* Four-class macro-F1 primary; per-class P/R/F1, accuracy, confusion matrix; paired bootstrap 95% CIs.
* Always report the shortcut baselines alongside LLM systems, and results **stratified by genre**
  (wire-news id block vs fact-check records), since the pooled number mixes two very different tasks.
* Silver evidence metrics only: own-article recall@k (document level) and explanation-sentence recall where the
  explanation occurs verbatim. Neither is semantic gold evidence.
